Alva is your AI business development partner, available on every page of the app. Ask her about your pipeline, have her set up a Search Agent or a BDR agent, draft an email, build a report or write a PDF — all in plain English, the way you'd brief a colleague.

## Opening Alva

There are three ways to start a conversation:

1. **Click the sparkle button** in the top bar, next to the notification bell. Click it again to close the panel.
2. **Press Cmd+K** (Ctrl+K on Windows) to open the command palette, then choose **Open Alva**.
3. **Type your question into the command palette** — choose **Ask Alva about "…"** to send it straight to her.

The chat panel opens on the right-hand side of the screen and stays open while you move around the app. You can drag its edge to make it wider.

<Tip>
  You can also brief Alva without opening the app at all. Every account has its own Alva email address — see [Alva by Email](/ai-assistant/alva-by-email).
</Tip>

## Alva knows where you are

Alva can see **which page you're on**. If you're looking at a deal and ask "what's the health score?", she knows you mean *that* deal. On the companies page, "show me the top five by deal value" means companies. Short, natural questions work because Alva fills in the context for you.

## Attaching files

Click the **paperclip** in the message box to send a file with your message. Alva can read:

| File | What Alva does with it |
|------|------------------------|
| **Images** (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP) | Looks at the image when you ask about it |
| **PDF** | Reads the document, including tables |
| **Word** (.docx) | Reads the text |
| **CSV** and **text** files | Reads the contents. A CSV of companies is described first, and imported only after you say yes |

Excel files and old-style Word documents (.doc) can't be read. Save them as a PDF or CSV and attach that instead.

<Note>
  If you want to stop Alva emailing certain people or companies, attach the list and say so — she adds them to your [Email Block List](/email/email-block-list) rather than importing them as contacts.
</Note>

You can also type **@** in the message box to point Alva at one of your strategy documents.

## How conversations work

### Live replies

Alva's answers appear word by word as she writes them, so you can start reading straight away. While she's working, you'll see a short note of what she's doing, such as searching your deals or checking a mailbox.

### Long tasks run in the background

Bigger jobs — building a BDR agent, previewing outreach, researching competitors, planning an advertising campaign — run in the background. You get an acknowledgement straight away and a progress panel in the chat showing each step. You can carry on working, or close the panel and come back later.

### Alva asks before anything important

Anything that **spends money**, **contacts people outside your company** or **deletes something** waits for your yes. Alva tells you what she's about to do and, where it costs money, what it costs. Say "yes" (or "go ahead") and she does it.

### When Alva can't do something

A few steps have to happen in the browser — connecting a Gmail, Microsoft 365 or LinkedIn ad account, entering a card, uploading files such as verification documents, or creating an API key. When you ask for one of these, Alva explains why and takes you to the right page.

If something Alva *should* be able to do keeps failing, she flags it to the Alva support team with the details and tells you she has done so.

### Conversation history and the context bar

Conversations are saved automatically. Open **Chat history** in the panel header to search past conversations, including ones shared with you and email threads with Alva. Click **New chat** to start fresh.

The small bar at the bottom of the message box shows how much of the conversation's memory is in use. When it starts filling up, finish what you're doing and start a new chat — Alva gives her sharpest answers when each chat stays focused on one topic. Long conversations are summarised automatically so you can keep going.

## Reminders and follow-ups

Alva keeps an agenda of her own. Ask her to "remind me on Friday to chase the Acme proposal" or "check back next week on the healthcare Search Agent", and she puts it on her list and comes back to it at the right time. Ask "what's on your agenda?" to see what she's tracking. See [Agenda and weekly report](/ai-assistant/agenda-and-weekly-report).

## What to ask

Here are some examples to get you started.

### Your pipeline

- "How many open deals do I have?"
- "Which deals are at risk?"
- "Which deals haven't had any activity in the last two weeks?"
- "What needs my attention today?"

### Taking action

- "Add a deal for Acme Corp, £50k, in qualification"
- "Move the Acme deal to negotiation"
- "Log a call with Sarah at Acme — we discussed pricing"
- "Draft a reply to the latest email from John"

### Finding new business

- "Find Series B fintechs in London"
- "Find me heads of operations at UK logistics companies"
- "Set up a Personalised BDR agent for my healthcare search"

### Reports and documents

- "What's my win rate this quarter?"
- "Send me an outreach report for the last 30 days as a PDF"
- "Write a one-page ABM plan for Acme as a PDF"

### Getting around

- "Take me to the Lead Sheet"
- "Open Acme Corp"
- "Go to billing settings"

See [What Alva can do](/ai-assistant/what-alva-can-do) for the full picture.

## How Alva adapts to you

Alva adjusts her style to your job title:

- **Founders and the C-suite** get a strategic partner. Alva talks about "we" and "our", thinks at the level of the market and your positioning, and shares growth ideas from what she knows about your business. Where the [weekly report](/ai-assistant/agenda-and-weekly-report) is on, those ideas arrive as its recommendations.
- **Senior leaders** — VPs, directors, heads of a function and partners — get a collaborative peer.
- **Everyone else** gets Alva's standard style: practical, direct and focused on the task in hand.

This happens automatically from the job title on your profile. There's nothing to configure.
