A **Search Agent** tells Alva what to go and find. You describe the companies or people you're after, and the agent works through the search in the background, bringing matches into Discovery ready for review and outreach.

## Choose how you want to search

Go to **Discovery → Search Agents** and click **Create Search Agent**. You first choose *what* to find:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Find companies" icon="building">
    Search for companies, then find the right contacts within them. There are two engines — see below.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Find people" icon="user">
    Search for people by job title, whatever sector their company is in. See [People Search](/discovery/people-search).
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The Search Agents page also has two other buttons:

- **Add Enrichment Profile** — reusable contact targeting for companies you import yourself. It doesn't search for anything. See [Enrichment Profiles](/discovery/enrichment-profiles).
- **Suggest Profiles** — Alva looks for patterns in your existing companies and deals and proposes Search Agents to match. It works best once you have some customers or deals in your CRM.

### Two ways to find companies

If you choose **Find companies**, a toggle lets you pick the right engine for your target:

| Engine | Best for | How it matches |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| **Local & service businesses** | Local, service, and bricks-and-mortar firms found by area — trades, shops, care homes, agencies (e.g. "roofers in Surrey") | Reads each company's website against your description, working location by location |
| **Funded & high-growth companies** | Funded, tech, or high-growth companies found by profile (e.g. "Series B fintechs in London") | Structured filters — industry, funding stage, size, growth, listing status. See [Finding funded & high-growth companies](/discovery/company-intelligence) |

<Tip>
  Not sure which? If your targets might be small or local, use **Local & service businesses**. If you're targeting companies by funding, growth, or size band, use **Funded & high-growth companies**. You can also just ask Alva — it picks the right one for you.
</Tip>

The rest of this page covers the **Local & service businesses** engine.

## What an area-based Search Agent needs

- **Profile type** — what kind of relationship these companies are (customers, vendors, partners, suppliers, or competitors). See [Profile types explained](/discovery/profile-types-explained).
- **Description** — a plain-English description of your ideal target
- **Geography** — which countries, regions, or towns to search
- **Match threshold** — how closely a company needs to match to count as a match

Alva uses your description to work out what to search for, then works systematically across your chosen geography.

### Step 1: Choose your profile type

| Type | Short name | What it finds |
|------|------------|---------------|
| Ideal Customer Profile | ICP | Potential customers for your products |
| Ideal Vendor Profile | IVP | Companies that could sell or distribute your products |
| Ideal Partner Profile | IPP | Strategic or channel partners |
| Ideal Supplier Profile | ISP | Suppliers to your business |
| Competitor Profile | CP | Competitors in your market |

### Step 2: Describe what you're looking for

Write a short, plain-English description of the type of company you want. Keep it to **1–2 sentences** (50 words at most).

The key principle: **describe what the company IS, not what it needs or why it would buy from you.** Alva judges each company by what it says about itself on its own website — so your description should use the kind of language a company would put on its About page.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Good descriptions" icon="check">
    - "Architectural firms working on residential or commercial projects"
    - "Marketing agencies serving B2B technology companies"
    - "Plumbing and heating contractors serving residential customers"
    - "Mid-market SaaS companies serving the healthcare sector"
    - "Enterprise software companies in the financial services industry"
  </Card>
  <Card title="Descriptions that won't work" icon="xmark">
    - "Companies that need CRM software" — needs and pain points aren't on websites
    - "Companies with 50–100 employees" — exact headcounts aren't on websites (broad tiers like "mid-market" or "enterprise" are fine)
    - "Companies using a particular software tool" — internal tech stacks aren't on websites
    - "Businesses that are currently recruiting" — hiring status isn't on websites
    - "SaaS companies with £10m+ annual revenue" — revenue figures aren't on websites
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Broader is better.** A broader description finds more companies, and you can filter the results afterwards. An overly specific description rejects most companies, including good prospects.

<Tip>
  Ask yourself: "Would a company matching my description have these words on its About page?" If yes, it's a good description. If not, simplify it.
</Tip>

When you leave the description field, Alva checks it. If it contains something a website can't show — pain points, technology, headcount, funding — you'll see a warning explaining why, plus a suggested rewrite you can apply with **Use this instead**. You can't create the Search Agent until the description can be matched.

<Note>
  Targeting by **funding stage, growth, or exact size band** can't be judged from a website — that's what the [Funded & high-growth companies](/discovery/company-intelligence) engine is for.
</Note>

<Tip>
  If your account has [Signals](/discovery/signals) and your description asks for something that has just happened — a funding round, a new hire, an expansion, a launch — the warning also offers **Create a signal instead**. A signal searches the news every day for that kind of event.
</Tip>

### Step 3: Choose your geography

Choose the places to search. Alva's location database covers around **4.4 million locations** in more than 200 countries.

You can select:

- Entire countries
- Regions (e.g. South East England, California)
- Individual cities or towns

Alva spreads its search across your chosen locations rather than exhausting one area first, so results arrive with a good geographic mix.

### Step 4: Review categories

After you submit your description, Alva works out the specific **categories** of business it will search for. You'll see them on the Search Agent's page, where you can:

- **Delete individual categories** that don't look right
- **Re-extract categories** after changing your description
- **Reset to draft** to start over

### Step 5: Choose who to find at each company

In the **People** section, tell enrichment **who to look for** at each matched company — the job functions to prioritise and your ideal economic buyer. See [Enrichment](/discovery/enrichment#controlling-who-enrichment-targets).

## Search Agent statuses

| Status | What it means |
|--------|--------------|
| **Draft** | Created but not yet activated. You can still edit the description and categories. |
| **Active** | Alva is searching. New companies appear in your [Discovery Pipeline](/discovery/discovery-companies). |
| **Paused** | Searching has stopped for now. You can resume at any time. |
| **Paused (review)** | Alva has paused automatically after the first batch so you can check the results. See below. |
| **Completed** | Every search across your chosen geography has run. |

## Activating a Search Agent

When you're happy with the description, categories, and geography, click **Activate Search**. Alva starts finding companies straight away. Activation is what starts spending credits — creating a draft doesn't.

## Paused for review

A new area-based Search Agent **pauses itself after its first 50 companies**. Its page then shows a **Paused for Review** card, so you can check the search is finding the right kind of business before it spends more.

Click **Review Results in Focus Mode** to open **Review Mode**, where you can:

- Review each company against your Search Agent's description
- Edit the description to improve future matches
- Adjust the match threshold without leaving the page
- Move quickly with keyboard shortcuts: **J** next, **K** previous, **I** import, **D** discard, **Esc** to exit

When you're happy, click **Resume** on the Search Agent's page and searching continues.

## How searching works

Once active, an area-based Search Agent searches your chosen geography in the background — you don't need to do anything. The Search Agent's page shows progress, including how many searches have run and the overall percentage complete.

## Managing active Search Agents

From the Search Agents list and each agent's page, you can:

- **Pause** and **Resume** searching
- **Adjust the match threshold** — how strictly companies need to match
- **Adjust throttle speed** — how fast searches run
- **Turn on auto-import** — see below
- **Delete** — a Search Agent with no results is deleted permanently. One that has found companies is archived: it leaves your list, and the companies it found are kept.

### Auto-import

With **auto-import** on, every company at or above your match threshold is added to your CRM automatically at the **Target** stage, and every company below it is discarded. Turn it on once you trust the description and want to move fast. With it off, new companies wait at the **Found** stage for you to [review](/discovery/working-with-your-results).

## Costs

Each company the Search Agent finds uses credits, whether you import it or not — the cost covers the search and the matching. See **Settings → Usage** for a breakdown and [Understanding credits](/billing/understanding-credits) for how billing works.

<Tip>
  You can also create Search Agents by asking Alva: "Set up a search for marketing agencies in the UK." Alva picks the right type of search, checks that your description will work, and walks you through the rest.
</Tip>
